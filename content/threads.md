### Essay 1 — Moderation Without the Lean

#### Truth Social thread

**1/** The biggest moderation risk at a heterodox platform is not a dangerous user. It is Apple or Google pulling your app.

**2/** Parler was fully operational on Friday. By Monday morning it did not exist. Apple, Google, and AWS each made a call. Total elapsed time: seventy-two hours.

**3/** Twitter, Reddit, Discord, and 4chan host content categories that would get a small platform pulled within a week. None of them have been pulled. They are bigger, not cleaner.

**4/** Apple's published guidelines do not say they apply a different standard to platforms with a perceived political valence. The record speaks anyway.

**5/** So what does a heterodox platform actually do? Moderate more aggressively on the categories Apple cares about. Not because those categories reflect your values. Because they are the conditions of your continued existence.

**6/** The vendor classifier is a commodity. It gives you a score. A 0.83 likelihood of harassment under the vendor's definition. The vendor's definition was written by people in San Francisco. You decide what to do with the number.

**7/** The lean does not live in the classifier. It lives in three things your team writes: the taxonomy, the thresholds, and the human policy document that tells reviewers what removable means.

**8/** Most platforms skip the policy document. They hire a head of trust and safety from a major platform and inherit a worldview with the resume.

**9/** A five-person team that has to encode everything into weights and thresholds is in a better position than a 500-person team that runs on shared culture. Encoded policy is inspectable. Inspectable is defensible.

**10/** When Apple asks why you handled a piece of content the way you did, the major platform points to culture. The small platform points to its config. The small platform wins that conversation more often than the press would have you believe.

**11/** The hard part is not the engineering. The hard part is sitting down and writing, in your own words, what your platform is for. Most platforms have not done this. The ones pulled from the App Store are the ones that did not.

#### LinkedIn post

The biggest moderation risk at a heterodox platform is not what users post. It is Apple or Google pulling your app.

Parler was fully operational on a Friday. By Monday morning it was gone. Apple, Google, and AWS each made a call. The total time from operational to offline was seventy-two hours.

The vendor classifier does not save you. It is a commodity. It gives you a score — 0.83 likelihood of harassment under a definition written by people in San Francisco. You decide what to do with 0.83. The lean does not live in the vendor.

It lives in three things your team writes.

First: the taxonomy. Which categories does your platform care about?

Second: the weights and thresholds. What does your platform do at 0.83? Auto-remove at 0.95. Ignore at 0.40. Between those, every number is a value judgment.

Third: the policy document your reviewers actually read. Most platforms skip this. They hire a head of trust and safety from a major platform and inherit a worldview with the resume.

A five-person team that has to encode everything into config is in a better position than a 500-person team that runs on culture. Encoded policy is inspectable. Inspectable is defensible.

When Apple asks why you handled a piece of content the way you did, the major platform points to outcomes and culture. The small platform points to its config.

The hard part is not engineering. It is writing down what your platform is for.
