#### LinkedIn post

The biggest moderation risk at a heterodox platform is not what users post. It is Apple or Google pulling your app.

Parler was fully operational on a Friday. By Monday morning it was gone. Apple, Google, and AWS each made a call. The total time from operational to offline was seventy-two hours.

The vendor classifier does not save you. It is a commodity. It gives you a score — 0.83 likelihood of harassment under a definition written by people in San Francisco. You decide what to do with 0.83. The lean does not live in the vendor.

It lives in three things your team writes.

First: the taxonomy. Which categories does your platform care about?

Second: the weights and thresholds. What does your platform do at 0.83? Auto-remove at 0.95. Ignore at 0.40. Between those, every number is a value judgment.

Third: the policy document your reviewers actually read. Most platforms skip this. They hire a head of trust and safety from a major platform and inherit a worldview with the resume.

A five-person team that has to encode everything into config is in a better position than a 500-person team that runs on culture. Encoded policy is inspectable. Inspectable is defensible.

When Apple asks why you handled a piece of content the way you did, the major platform points to outcomes and culture. The small platform points to its config.

The hard part is not engineering. It is writing down what your platform is for.

### Essay 2 — What the deplatforming of Parler actually taught us

**5/** The lesson from Parler is not "moderate harder." It is: do not depend on infrastructure run by people who hate you.

#### LinkedIn post

Parler was fully operational on January 8, 2021. AWS notified them on January 10 they'd be cut off at midnight. By Monday morning the platform was gone. Seventy-two hours.

The official reason was January 6 moderation. Facebook hosted vastly more of the actual organizing, by orders of magnitude. None of the majors lost a hosting contract. The deplatforming had a pretext. It did not have a cause.

The cause was simpler. AWS made a political calculation. They decided the cost of removing a conservative-coded platform during a national news cycle was zero. No contract lost. No stock movement. They were right.

Four cases show the decision tree clearly.

Parler: trusted AWS. Gone.

Gab: total refusal. Andrew Torba chose no app stores, no payment processors, self-hosting. Gab still exists. Its user base is in the low hundreds of thousands. That is the price of total refusal.

Rumble: Chris Pavlovski built his own video pipeline, diversified payment providers and CDNs, and accepted minimum app-store accommodation. Tens of millions of monthly users. A public listing. The middle path works.

Telegram: opacity buys latitude. A platform useful to enough state actors gets treated differently. Not a model most Western founders can replicate.

The operating principle: own the core. Partner only with operators whose politics make destroying you a cost rather than a benefit. Treat every major infrastructure dependency as a known vulnerability. Build the contingency before you launch.

The infrastructure was never neutral.

#### LinkedIn post

The biggest moderation problem at a heterodox platform is not technical. It is this: every moderation decision is a values call, and the people the industry sends you to hire do not have values that are intelligible to your users.

Bay Area trust and safety engineers are competent. The problem is not competence. The problem is that their working moral framework, when pressed, reduces to what they personally feel is harmful plus what their peers also feel is harmful. That is not a moral compass. It is a weather vane.

A moral compass requires a standard outside yourself. One you did not invent. One you cannot revise when it becomes inconvenient. Christians have one. People formed in serious religious traditions have one. People who came up through military or law enforcement have a version of one. The Bay Area's professional class, by and large, does not.

The failure mode is not openly hostile. The engineer shows up, is pleasant, and does the work. The taxonomy they propose maps to Bay Area concerns. The thresholds are calibrated to Bay Area sensibilities. The reviewer training treats the speech your users care about preserving as borderline-at-best. None of this is sabotage. It is sincerely held. Six months in, you have a moderation system that reads like a slower-moving version of every major platform. You paid premium rates to import the exact problem you started the platform to solve.

The alternative pool exists. It is small and outside the standard pipelines. Senior engineers with moral frameworks from outside themselves — Reformed, Catholic, Orthodox, Evangelical, military and law enforcement backgrounds. People who can tell you what they believe and why. People who can recognize a hard call as hard rather than obvious.

You cannot outsource your conscience to people who do not have one of their own.
