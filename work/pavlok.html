01

The context

Software becomes tangible when you wear it.

Pavlok pairs wearable devices with a mobile app for habits and routines. Its current product material describes sound, vibration, and electrical feedback, along with integrations such as IFTTT and Zapier. Explore Pavlok’s product ecosystem ↗

02

My role

As Head of Software, I led software for Pavlok’s habit-changing wearable and scaled the engineering team to more than 30 people. That experience brings product engineering and team leadership together in one part of my background.

03

The engineering problem

A connected product asks several systems to deliver one coherent experience. The person wearing it encounters the result as a whole. That makes the boundaries between components—and the people responsible for them—an important engineering consideration.

Engineering consideration

Sending a command and confirming an action are different events. Disconnections, retries, and stale state are useful questions to ask of any connected product. This diagram is a general design lens, not a reconstruction of Pavlok’s internal architecture.

04 / A useful lesson

The handoffs are
part of the system.

A team needs shared definitions of what happened, what happens next, and who owns the exception. The same is true of an automated workflow. Clear boundaries help both people and software work together.
